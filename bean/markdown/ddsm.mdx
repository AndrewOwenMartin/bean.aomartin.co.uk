# The Discrete Dynamical Systems model of SDS

This analysis assumes the search space contains exactly one hypothesis with a quality larger than any other hypothesis, called the optimal hypothesis, and many hypotheses with a significantly lower score, the collective effect of which can be described by a single value known collectively as homogeneous background noise.

Introduced in [60] and explored in [62], the Discrete Dynamical System Model (DDSM) of SDS has been used to clearly express results previously reached via other methods as well as new models of the behaviour of SDS. The DDSM introduces the following notation.

- $\alpha$ the score of the optimal hypothesis
- $\beta$ the mean probability of an agent at any non-optimal hypothesis becoming active
- $\bar{c}_i$ the mean number of active agents maintaining the optimal hypothesis as a proportion
  of the total population
- $S$ the search space size, the number of potential hypotheses
  in a search space
- $A$ the swarm size, the number of agents in a swarm
- $\text{global activity}$ The proportion of the swarm which are active at a given time

In Standard SDS, after the test phase, if you have:

- a swarm of $S$ agents.
- a cluster of $c$ active agents.
- a noise of $\beta$
  There will be
- $\frac{1-a}{a}c$ inactive at the cluster
- $\left[1-b\right]\left[1-\frac{c}{a}\right]$ inactive with noise hypotheses
- $b(\frac{1-c}{a})$ active with noise hypotheses
- $\frac{c}{a}$ active and inactive at the cluster
- $1-\frac{c}{a}$ active and inactive with noise hypotheses

The cluster will be stable when leaving = arriving.

Arriving = diffusing in + hypothesising in

diffusing in = total inactive * active at cluster

= $\left(\frac{1-a}{a}c + \left[1-b\right]\left[1-\frac{c}{a}\right]\right)c$

Leaving = going inactive = $c(1-a)$

Simplfies to $(b-a)(\frac{c}{a} - 1) = 0$
or
$(b-a)(c-a) = 0$

Where either a=b or a=c
