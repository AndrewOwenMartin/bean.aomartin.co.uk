# Context-free SDS: DDSM results

Cleaned restatement of `thesis/003.4-variants-of-sds.md` §3.4.3.1.

## Assumptions

Same DDSM assumptions as standard SDS (see [Standard SDS summary](/standard-sds-summary)):

- Exactly one hypothesis has a score higher than all others (the **optimal hypothesis**).
- All other hypotheses' collective effect is modelled as a single value, **homogeneous
  background noise**.
- $S$ — search space size. $A$ — swarm size.
- $\alpha$ — score of the optimal hypothesis. $\beta$ — mean probability of an agent at any
  non-optimal hypothesis becoming active. $\bar{c}_i$ — mean proportion of the population
  that are active agents maintaining the optimal hypothesis.

Context-free diffusion additionally assumes agents at the noise hypothesis never become
inactive during the diffusion phase — an imperfect assumption, since diffusion causes noise
agents to duplicate identical hypotheses.

## One-step evolution function

$$\bar{c}_{i+1} = \bar{c}_i\big[2\alpha(1-\beta)\big] - \bar{c}_i^{\,2}\big[2(\alpha-\beta)\big]$$

## Minimum convergence criterion

$$\alpha_{\min} = \frac{1}{2(1-\beta)}$$

## Robustness

$$\zeta = 1 - \ln 2 \approx 0.307$$

Half the robustness of standard SDS: the global collision cap keeps clusters smaller, and
smaller clusters need a stronger signal to hold together.

## Steady state

$$\gamma = \frac{2\alpha(1-\beta) - 1}{2(\alpha-\beta)}$$

## What it caps

Context-free diffusion caps the **total number of active agents in the swarm** (a global
cap). Every active agent that polls another active agent — regardless of hypothesis — is
sent back to `DH()`. See [Context-free and context-sensitive SDS](/context-free-sensitive)
for the diffusion mechanism and worked comparison with standard and context-sensitive SDS.
